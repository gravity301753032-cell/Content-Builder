# Bezos Doesn't Care About Being #1. Neither Should You.

In *Creating Wealth*, Jeff Bezos wrote:

*"People are naturally curious about the title of 'world's richest person,' but I never pursued it. When I was ranked #2, I was perfectly at ease."*

He wasn't being humble. He was revealing how he sees the world: **as a game where the leaderboard doesn't matter.**

---

Here's a framework I've been developing.

The world is a game. Everyone else is an NPC.

Not literally — psychologically.

When you're jealous of someone's success, ask yourself: would you be jealous of an NPC? When you're lying awake at night worrying what people think of you, ask: would you care what an NPC thinks?

The moment you see others as non-player characters, their judgment loses all power. Their opinions become background noise. You stop competing with people who were never playing your game to begin with.

Naval Ravikant says the same thing: the world is a game. Every day you make decisions — just like choosing your loadout in GTA. The mechanics are identical. The only difference is the consequences are real.

---

This sounds cold. But it's the opposite of nihilism. It's freedom.

Because once NPC judgment stops mattering, you can focus entirely on what actually matters: **leveling up your character.**

Bezos didn't argue about whether capitalism is good or bad. He learned the rules. Elon Musk doesn't donate to charity — he uses capitalism to solve problems at planetary scale.

You can't change the game engine. You can change your character.

Every book is a skill point. Every hard conversation is experience. Every failure is a respawn with more knowledge than last time.

---

One last thing.

What if your own monkey mind — that voice that triggers jealousy, anxiety, self-doubt — is just an NPC command? A script running in your head that someone else wrote?

Awareness is the cheat code. The moment you observe the thought instead of obeying it, you're not an NPC anymore.

You're a player.

Ignore the leaderboard. Ignore the NPCs. Equip your skills. Play your game.
