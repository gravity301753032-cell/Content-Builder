**The Most Important Investing Lesson I Learned This Week (It's Not About Stocks)**

I've been reading Jeff Bezos's 2000 shareholder letter. Amazon's stock had just dropped 80%. The dot-com bubble had burst. Everyone was calling Amazon a failure.

But Bezos opened his letter with a quote from Benjamin Graham (Warren Buffett's mentor):

*"In the short term, the stock market is a voting machine; in the long term, it's a weighing machine."*

Then he listed the numbers. Customers: 14M → 20M. Sales: $1.64B → $2.76B. Operating loss shrinking from 26% to 6% of sales in Q4 alone. By every fundamental metric, the company was the strongest it had ever been.

The market was voting. And the vote was wrong.

---

I have an accounting background. I spent years learning to read financial statements. I can tell you exactly how to calculate operating margins and cash conversion cycles. But this letter taught me something my CPA exams never did:

The market's vote has nothing to do with your weight.

This applies far beyond investing. I'm 28, building a startup, no stable income yet. By society's voting machine — salary, title, status — I'm not exactly crushing it.

But I know what I'm building. I know the analysis skills compounding daily. I know the frameworks I'm internalizing from reading Bezos, Naval, and the Stoics. The weighing machine doesn't care about my current bank balance. It cares about the weight I'm putting on every day.

---

Bezos ended that letter with a line I haven't stopped thinking about:

*"We're a company that wants to be weighed, and over time, we will be."*

The voting machine is temporary. The weighing machine is permanent. Focus on your weight.
