# LinkedIn Post

**You'll Never See a Failed Founder Write a Book Called "Long-Term Thinking"**

I've been reading Jeff Bezos's shareholder letters and came across his Princeton story — the moment he quit theoretical physics because he realized he might never be in the world's top 50.

It's a classic founder narrative. Identify your limits. Pivot. Succeed.

But here's what nobody talks about.

---

For every Jeff Bezos who quit physics and built Amazon, there are thousands of founders who made the same kind of rational pivot — and failed anyway.

They read Benjamin Graham. They obsessed over customers. They embraced long-term thinking. They worked harder than anyone. And their companies still died.

You will never read their books. You will never see their TED talks. Because survivorship bias erases them from history.

**We don't learn from successful people. We learn from the 1% of successful people who were lucky enough to survive — and then we reverse-engineer their decisions into "wisdom."**

---

This doesn't mean you shouldn't study Bezos. I do it every day. His thinking on capital allocation, customer obsession, and operational discipline is genuinely world-class.

But I try to read with one question in the back of my mind:

*"Is this actually true — or did it just happen to work out for this one guy?"*

The difference matters. Because if you blindly copy a survivor's playbook, you're betting your life on someone else's luck.

Study the survivors. But remember the invisible 99% who did the same things and didn't make it.
