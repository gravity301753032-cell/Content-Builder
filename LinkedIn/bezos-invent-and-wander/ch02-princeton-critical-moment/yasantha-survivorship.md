# LinkedIn Post

**Bezos Said This Guy Was the Smartest Person at Princeton. Then History Forgot Him.**

In his 2018 talk at the Economic Club of Washington, Jeff Bezos told a story about a Princeton classmate named Yasantha Rajakarunanayake.

Bezos and his roommate had spent three hours trying to solve a partial differential equation. They couldn't crack it. They brought it to Yasantha.

He looked at it. Thought for a moment. Then said: "Cosine."

He was right. He'd solved it entirely in his head.

Bezos said that was the moment he realized he'd never be a top-tier theoretical physicist. So he quit physics and switched to electrical engineering and computer science. The rest is history.

---

It's a perfect "know your limits" story. Inspiring. Actionable. Very LinkedIn.

But here's what nobody talks about: **What happened to Yasantha?**

I looked him up.

Yasantha Rajakarunanayake got his PhD from Caltech. He holds over 100 patents. He's spent decades as a researcher, mathematician, and AI scientist at companies like MediaTek. By any objective measure, he's had an extraordinarily successful career.

But you've never heard of him.

The only reason anyone knows his name is because Bezos mentioned it once in a speech. After that talk, Yasantha's LinkedIn inbox exploded. Strangers kept asking the same question: *"Are you Jeff Bezos's Yasantha?"*

His Wikipedia page — yes, he has one — carries a warning banner: "This article may not meet Wikipedia's notability guidelines."

Think about that for a second.

---

Bezos looked at Yasantha and thought: *This guy is smarter than me. He'll contribute more to the world than I ever will.*

But history didn't work that way.

The guy Bezos thought was too smart to fail at physics ended up being remembered only because Bezos mentioned him. And the guy who quit because he felt inadequate ended up being the one history wrote books about.

This isn't just a story about two Princeton classmates. It's survivorship bias made flesh.

**We don't learn from the smartest people. We learn from the luckiest survivors — and then we call it wisdom.**

---

Yasantha isn't a failure. He's objectively successful. But he's invisible to history. And that should humble anyone who thinks there's a formula for success.

Because for every Jeff Bezos, there's a Yasantha who was smarter, worked harder, and still didn't become a billionaire.

Study the survivors. But remember the invisible ones.
