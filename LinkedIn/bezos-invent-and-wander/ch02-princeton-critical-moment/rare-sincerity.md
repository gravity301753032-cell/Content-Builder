**The Most Underrated Part of Jeff Bezos's Origin Story**

Everyone knows the Amazon origin story. Bezos quit his Wall Street job, drove across the country, and started an online bookstore in his garage.

But there's a detail in that story that almost nobody talks about.

Before Bezos quit, he told his boss. His boss at DE Shaw — a man named David Shaw — didn't panic about KPIs. Didn't calculate replacement costs. Didn't try to manipulate him into staying.

He took Bezos for a long walk in Central Park. Listened to the entire idea. And then said:

*"This is a really good idea. But I think it's a better idea for someone who doesn't already have a good job. Take 48 hours. Think about it. Then decide."*

---

Think about that for a second.

Bezos was a valuable employee. His departure would create a real loss for the firm. Shaw had every incentive to protect his own interests. Instead, he genuinely considered what was best for Bezos.

How many bosses have you had who would do that?

I've had managers who chose the convenient lie over the uncomfortable truth. Who prioritized protecting themselves over being honest with me. It's not that they were bad people — it's that most workplace relationships are fundamentally transactional.

David Shaw transcended the transaction. He treated Bezos not as an asset to be retained, but as a human being making the biggest decision of his life.

---

We spend so much time analyzing Bezos's decisions. His Day 1 philosophy. His long-term thinking. His customer obsession.

But part of his success was simply this: at a critical moment, he had someone who was genuinely in his corner. Someone who chose kindness over cleverness.

Not everyone gets that. If you find someone like David Shaw in your career — appreciate them. They're rarer than you think.
