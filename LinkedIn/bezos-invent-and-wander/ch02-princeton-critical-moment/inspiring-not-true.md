# LinkedIn Post — Inspiring ≠ True

**Jeff Bezos Gave Princeton Graduates 10 Questions. Here's What Nobody Talks About.**

At the end of his 2010 Princeton commencement speech, Jeff Bezos posed ten questions:

*Will you follow dogma, or be original? Will you choose a life of ease, or a life of service and adventure? Will you wilt under criticism, or follow your convictions?*

Every question follows the same structure: the first option is cautious and conventional. The second is bold and inspiring. Bezos clearly wants you to pick the second.

The speech is brilliant. It's earned its place as one of the most shared commencement addresses of all time.

But here's what nobody mentions.

---

There are probably 99 people who *did* follow their passions. Who *did* choose originality over conformity. Who *did* pick adventure over ease. Who *were* kind instead of clever.

And they still failed.

Their companies died. Their projects flopped. Their convictions led them into dead ends. They did everything Bezos said to do — and it didn't work.

You will never hear their commencement speeches. Because survivorship bias erases them from history. We only get to hear from the 1% who survived — and then we call their hindsight "wisdom."

---

This doesn't mean Bezos is wrong. Passion, conviction, and kindness *are* better than apathy, conformity, and cruelty. They're worth pursuing for their own sake.

But they're not a success formula. They're a survivor's narrative.

The real lesson of Bezos's speech isn't "do these ten things and you'll succeed." It's: *do these ten things because they define who you are — regardless of the outcome.*

Because the 99 who failed also lived meaningful lives. They just didn't get to give the speech.

---

Study the survivors. But remember the invisible ones. Their stories matter too.
