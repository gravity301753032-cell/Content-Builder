**"It's Harder to Be Kind Than Clever" — What Jeff Bezos's Grandfather Taught Me About Leadership**

In his 2010 Princeton commencement speech, Jeff Bezos told a story I can't stop thinking about.

He was ten years old, on a road trip with his grandparents. His grandmother was smoking in the front seat. Young Jeff, proud of his math skills, calculated how many cigarettes she smoked per day, how many minutes each took off her life, and announced from the back seat:

*"At two minutes per puff, you've taken nine years off your life."*

His grandmother burst into tears.

His grandfather pulled the car over. Got out. Walked around. Opened Jeff's door. And in the silence of that roadside moment, said something Bezos has carried for thirty years:

**"Jeff, one day you'll understand that it's harder to be kind than clever."**

---

Bezos later reflected: cleverness is a gift. Kindness is a choice. Gifts are easy — they're given to you. Choices are hard. If you're not careful, you'll let your gifts blind you to what your choices are actually doing.

I think about this all the time in business contexts. How many meetings have I walked out of where someone chose to be clever — the perfect rebuttal, the airtight logic — instead of kind? How many times have I done it myself?

Cleverness is the default. It requires no effort. Kindness requires you to pause. To think about the person on the other side. To choose the harder path.

Bezos's grandfather didn't teach him to be less smart. He taught him that being smart isn't enough.
