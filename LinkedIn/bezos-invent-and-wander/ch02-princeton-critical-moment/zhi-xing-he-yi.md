# LinkedIn Post — 知行合一

**Wang Yangming, Jeff Bezos's Grandfather, and the Gap Between Knowing and Doing**

There's a concept in Chinese philosophy called 知行合一 (zhī xíng hé yī) — the unity of knowledge and action. The 16th-century philosopher Wang Yangming argued that if you truly *know* what is right, you will inevitably *do* it. If you don't do it, you never truly knew it in the first place.

I used to think this was abstract philosophy. Then two things made it real.

---

The first: Bezos's grandfather.

When ten-year-old Jeff told his grandmother she'd shortened her life by nine years (he'd calculated it mathematically), his grandfather didn't lecture him. Didn't punish him. He simply said: *"It's harder to be kind than clever."*

He knew what kindness looked like. And he did it. Quietly. On the side of a highway. With no audience. That's 知行合一 in its purest form.

---

The second: a personal experience.

I once left a role where the reason given for my departure didn't match what happened next. Within two days, the same role was re-posted with nearly identical responsibilities. The real reason wasn't shared with me — not out of malice, I suspect, but because honesty in that moment required more discomfort than the alternative.

This person *knew* honesty was the right thing. But knowing and doing are different things.

---

Wang Yangming's insight is devastatingly simple: every time you choose clever over kind, you move one step further from true understanding. Every time you choose kind over clever — especially when it's uncomfortable — you move one step closer.

Bezos's grandfather was 知行合一. Most of us are still working on it.
