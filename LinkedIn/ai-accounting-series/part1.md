Part 1 of 5: What this means for your finance team.

Two founders of an accounting software company ran 22 AI models through 101 real accounting tasks. Nestares: "finance doesn't run on drafts, it runs on validated records." Dohmen: "on accounting work that trade is mostly gone, what is left is consistency."

The best model got 92 of the 101 right, on things like classify this transaction, write this journal entry, match this bank line. An open model got 89, at six-tenths of a cent a task. The winner was never trained for accounting.

Accounting has one acceptable answer, and a close is not one question, it is hundreds in a row. At 91% per question, the chance every question across a 200-question close is right is about one in a hundred million.

Consistency decides whether you can sign. Each question ran three times. 92 were right at least twice. Only 87 were right all three times. On about five, the model was right twice and wrong once, and from the output alone you cannot tell which run you got.

So at 91% you still need a person on every judgement call. That is not a bug, it is arithmetic.

Picture the best accountant in the world being hired. They have read every textbook, every standard, every working paper. You still would not let them close your books, because they have never worked in your industry and never seen your company.

The model is that person. It knows debit and credit. It does not know you recognise revenue on accrual basis, what your capitalisation threshold is, how you depreciate. It applies the textbook treatment, confidently, and the gap between the textbook answer and your answer is the whole company.

Some of that gap no rule will ever close, because it is not ignorance, it is judgement. Two good accountants can disagree on a modified contract. That part is permanent human work.

So what does the profession need? Not AI expertise. Specification. AI does not create a new skill, it makes an old one scarce again: writing down what you actually do, so a machine can execute it and a reviewer can re-perform it. Most teams discover here that their policy is written down nowhere except in people's heads.

Where this goes is a general model with your own data attached, and as the cost of running a model on your own data falls, more of it moves in-house.

And AI will keep getting better. Cheaper, faster, more accurate. That is the easy part. The gap between a general model and your business closes when your accounting is written down, not when the next model ships.

Which part of your team's work is already solved by AI, and which part still needs a person in ten years?
