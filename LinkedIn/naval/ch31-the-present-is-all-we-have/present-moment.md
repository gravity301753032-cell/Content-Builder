# The Only Thing You Actually Control — LinkedIn

Parts 1 and 2 covered meaning and values. Here's where it gets uncomfortable.

Naval's Chapter 31 is titled *The Present Is All We Have*. It's a short chapter — barely a few pages — but it lands hard.

The idea is simple: the past is gone. The future doesn't exist yet. The only thing that's ever real is right now. The present moment.

Easy to nod at. Harder to actually live.

---

I kept thinking about this in the context of everything we've been discussing. We talk about "playing long-term games" and "building compound interest" like they're guarantees. Eat healthy, exercise, build reputation, compound trust — and the returns will come.

But will they?

Here's a real example. Someone I know spent years obsessing over health. Gym every morning. Tracked every meal. Did everything right. Then got diagnosed with cancer out of nowhere.

Another story: a friend of a friend, perfectly healthy, crossed the street at the wrong moment and never made it home.

Were they playing the "wrong" long-term game? No. They did everything right. The future just didn't cooperate.

---

This is the part Naval doesn't say explicitly, but it's there if you read between the lines: **long-term thinking is a process, not a guarantee.**

You can't control outcomes. You can only control inputs. And you can only control them one moment at a time.

An hour is just 60 minutes stacked together. A year is 8,760 hours. "Long-term" isn't a thing that exists somewhere in the future waiting for you. It's just a lot of present moments, made well, one after another.

The health example really crystallized this for me. Exercise isn't a guarantee against illness. It's a guarantee that you spent this hour doing something good for yourself. The benefit might be future health. Or it might just be that you felt strong today. Either way, the moment itself wasn't wasted.

---

Steve Jobs famously said: if today were the last day of your life, would you want to do what you're about to do?

If tomorrow were the end of the world, nobody would be thinking about long-term strategy. That doesn't mean you go out and do something destructive. It means: not everything has room for long-term thinking. Sometimes the only thing you have is right now.

---

This connects back to what I wrote in the first two posts.

Naval's three answers to the meaning of life eventually point to this: there's no cosmic meaning, but your existence is meaning. His values framework gives you a decision engine — a way to navigate choices without spiraling. But the engine only works in the present. You can't make decisions in the past. You can't make decisions in the future. You can only make them now.

And here's the synthesis that ties it all together:

**When you're clear on your values, and your actions match your inner state — the 知行合一 that Wang Yangming described — you don't need to agonize over every decision. Your values become your auto-pilot. Long-term and short-term stop being categories you have to think about. The only question that matters in each moment is: am I being true to myself right now?**

Of course, values evolve. You'll go through things that change what you believe. The framework you build today won't be the same one you use ten years from now. That's not a flaw. That's the game updating its rules as you level up.

There's no absolute answer. Never was.

---

I've been thinking about a line from The School of Hard Knocks: *you are the decisions of your past.* When you look backward and connect the dots, every choice you made — long-term, short-term, impulsive, calculated — it all became who you are.

You can make short-term choices your whole life and be fine — right up until one of them costs you too much and you can't play anymore. You can grind nothing but long-term quests and wake up at 60 realizing you missed most of the game.

Neither path is wrong. Both come with a bill.

The only thing you actually control is this moment. And in this moment, the only thing you need to ask is: *after this choice, can I still play tomorrow?*
